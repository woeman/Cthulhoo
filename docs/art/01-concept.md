## A plate that goes mad

Imagine an expedition report from the 1920s. A scientist lowers a steel sphere on a
cable into the open ocean and records what she sees through a quartz porthole. Her
report is illustrated with careful copperplate engravings: fine parallel lines, cross
hatching for shadow, a creature's outline drawn with total confidence.

Now imagine the report was found on the sea floor, and the deeper the page went, the
less it could be trusted. The lines start to glow. The hatching turns at angles that
are almost right. The creature in the engraving is too big for the frame, and part of
it is missing. A letter in the caption is not a letter.

That is Cthulhoo. Every day you answer one question and sink. The look follows you
down, from a sober scientific record at the surface to something dreaming at 7,000 m.

## The five rules

### 1. Ink becomes light

At the surface everything is dark ink on paper. Below about 200 m the page inverts and
the same lines become light, the colour of the zone's living things. Never draw a new
style for the deep. Draw the same engraving and let it glow.

- Do: keep line art, hatching and the plate frames identical above and below; only the
  colours swap.
- Don't: add neon, gradients for their own sake, or a "sci-fi" glow that has no line under it.

### 2. Science first, then dread

The game starts as an honest field record: depth readings, pressure, temperature, Latin
names, a typewritten log. Horror creeps in through small errors, never through gore.

- Do: real units (metres, atmospheres, degrees Celsius), real sea creatures, real ocean zones.
- Don't: blood, skulls, jump scares, cartoon monsters.

### 3. Show less

The scariest creature is the one you only partly see. Never show a whole monster.

- Do: an eye in the dark, a limb crossing the edge of the frame, a shape against a lamp,
  the scale of a thing implied by the marine snow drifting past it.
- Don't: a full-body monster, a creature lit from the front, a creature centred in frame.

### 4. Nothing bounces

Everything moves as if it were deep under water: slow, heavy, damped. Idle things breathe
once every seven seconds.

- Do: long eases, slow drifts, things that sink when they leave.
- Don't: springs, wobbles, elastic overshoot, confetti.

### 5. The wrong angle

The geometry of the deep is almost regular. A frame is a degree off true. A star has seven
points, not five or six. A hatch runs at an angle no engraver would choose. The stone of the
Drowned City leans at angles that do not agree with each other.

- Do: odd numbers, near-symmetry, slight asymmetry that you only notice the second time.
- Don't: obvious chaos. Wrongness must be quiet to be unsettling.

## The Rule of Seven

Seven is the game's private number. It ties the pieces together without anyone being told:

- the floor is at 7,000 m;
- idle things breathe on a 7-second cycle;
- the Arcane seal has seven spines, the Forbidden and Unspeakable seals are seven-pointed stars;
- the sounding line (the depth gauge) is marked every 500 m: fourteen marks, twice seven.

Use seven when you need a number and nothing else decides it.

## The descent as a story

| Depth | What changes |
|---|---|
| 0 m, The Shallows | Paper and ink. Sun rays through the water, fog, caustics. Everything is calm and scientific. |
| 200 m, The Gloaming | The ink wash rises and the page inverts. The bathysphere lamp is the only warm light: amber. |
| 1,000 m, The Stygian Dark | True dark. Cyan sparks of living light. Marine snow drifts upward past you. Something with suckers crosses the far water. |
| 4,000 m, The Cyclopean Abyss | Stone appears: monoliths that lean, masonry joined by glowing cracks. Green light. The text begins to go wrong. |
| 6,000 m, The Dreaming Deep | Magenta, the colour out of space. The ruins are everywhere. Letters become other letters. |
| 7,000 m, The Drowned City | The lines draw an Eye with a cuttlefish's W-shaped pupil, and it opens. Its name is written in glyphs. |

## Moodboard words

Copperplate. Foxing. Bathysphere. Quartz porthole. Pressure. Lamp failing. Marine snow.
Phosphorescence. Radiolarian. Specimen tag. Telegram. Leaning stone. Sleeper. Patience.

## How we stay original

Cthulhoo's mechanic (rarer answers go deeper) is a familiar daily-game idea and is fine to
share. Everything you can see or hear must be ours:

- Our references are old and public: 19th-century engraving, Ernst Haeckel's plates of
  radiolarians and jellyfish (published 1904), deep-sea expedition reports, 1930s
  bathysphere dives. None of our art is traced or copied from them; they are a mood.
- We never borrow another game's palette, layout, iconography, typeface pairing or wording.
- The cosmic-horror idea (a vast, ancient, indifferent deep) is ours to use. Specific
  characters and texts from Lovecraft's stories are not used, except four creature names in
  the bestiary (see [Voice](08-voice.md)). Our sleeping thing is simply "the Sleeper", our
  city is "the Drowned City", and its name is four glyphs.
