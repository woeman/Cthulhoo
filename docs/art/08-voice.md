# 8. Voice

[Back to the style bible](README.md)

## Who is speaking

The game speaks as **the expedition**: a calm, precise scientist of the 1920s, her typed log,
and the telegraph operator on the ship above. They are brave, dry and exact. They never joke
about the deep, and they never describe the horror directly. The dread is in what they leave
out, and in the moments where their record goes slightly wrong.

**Guess:** the scientist is never named on screen. She is "the expedition" and "we".

## Three registers

| Register | Voice | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The record** | plain, warm, exact | questions, rules, results, settings | "Name a creature that lives below a thousand metres." |
| **The log** | typed, terse, with times and readings | the dive log, loading, history | "04:12 · 4,312 m · 430 atm · 2.6 °C · Lamp failing." |
| **The telegram** | capitals, no punctuation, STOP between phrases | toasts and notices from the ship | "RARE ANSWER RECEIVED STOP PAYING OUT CABLE STOP" |

And one more, used very sparingly: **the deep**, which speaks only in italics, only below
4,000 m, only in a few words. "*Come down.*" "*We remember you.*" Never more than one line.

## Sample copy

**First visit:**
> Every day the deep asks one question with many true answers. Common answers keep you
> near the surface. Rare ones let out more cable. How deep will you go?

**Results, by deepest tier:**

- Commonplace: "A pleasant day on the water."
- Curious: "A little further from shore than most."
- Obscure: "Known to lanternfish and few others."
- Arcane: "Carved, not written."
- Forbidden: "You should not know this."
- Unspeakable: "The deep repeats it back to you."
- Reached the floor: "You have reached the floor. It has been waiting."

**Telegrams:**

- "ANSWER RECEIVED STOP PAYING OUT CABLE STOP"
- "LANTERN STILL LIT STOP TWELVE DAYS STOP"
- "NEW DESCENT AT DAWN STOP"
- "SIGNAL LOST STOP" (offline)

**Errors**, in the record's voice, always clear about what to do:

- "The line has gone quiet. Check your connection and try again."
- "That answer is already in today's log."

## Rules

- Short sentences. Plain words. British spelling (metres, colour), because the expedition's
  report reads that way. **Guess:** swap to American spelling if the game's market wants it; it
  is one decision for the whole game.
- Real units, always: metres, atmospheres, degrees Celsius.
- No gore, no swearing, no memes, no "spooky" puns, no exclamation marks.
- The deep never explains itself.
- **Cosmic horror, not Lovecraft's prejudices.** Lovecraft's stories carry racism and other
  bigotry. None of that belongs here. The horror is the ocean's size, age and indifference, never
  a people, a culture or a religion. Do not name real religions, real peoples or real places as
  sources of evil.
- **Our own names, with four exceptions.** We do not use Cthulhu, R'lyeh, the Necronomicon or other
  named characters, places or books from Lovecraft's fiction in the game. The game's own name plays
  on the idea; the game's world is ours: the Sleeper, the Drowned City, the Drowned Script. The
  exceptions are four creatures of the old stories in the bestiary, named because the game's
  feature list asks for them (27 November 2026): Father Dagon, Mother Hydra, the shoggoth and the
  Deep Ones. Party codes such as DAGON-7 may use them too. They are drawn as sea creatures, never as
  a people, and none of Lovecraft's lore about bloodlines or "tainted" towns comes with them. The
  bestiary's Dreamer is our own Sleeper, seen only as its eye. **Guess:** these names are reused
  across many games and books; keep them out of the game's name, logo and store listing.
