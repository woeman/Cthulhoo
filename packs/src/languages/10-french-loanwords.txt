6 Mayday | May day :: Coined in 1923 by a Croydon radio officer for pilots crossing the Channel. It is the French "m'aider", help me, spelled the way an Englishman hears it.
5 Mortgage :: Old French for "dead pledge". The deal dies either when the debt is paid or when the payments stop.
6 Curfew :: From the Old French "couvre-feu", cover the fire: the evening bell that told medieval townsfolk to bank their hearths so the town would not burn overnight.
5 Dandelion :: From "dent de lion", lion's tooth, for its jagged leaves. The French themselves now call it pissenlit, which is rather less noble.
5 Denim :: Serge "de Nîmes": cloth from the French town of Nîmes.
5 Tennis :: Probably from "tenez!", meaning "take this!", which French players called out as they served.
